v1.2Aug 2026

Building my first Active Directory home lab

What went right, what broke during the domain join, and why GPOs are more forgiving than they look.

Read more →

I wanted to actually build the thing I'd been reading about, not just pass a quiz on it. So I spun up Windows Server 2022 in VirtualBox and promoted it to a domain controller — which is a fancy way of saying I told a fresh install of Windows Server "you're in charge of a network now."

The forest and DNS came up cleanly. The DHCP scope was mostly typing in a range and setting a lease time. The part that actually taught me something was joining a Windows 11 client to the domain — it kept failing, and the error messages were vague enough that I had to slow down and check DNS settings on the client instead of the server. Once the client was pointed at the domain controller's IP for DNS, the join went through on the first try.

After that I built out Organizational Units and user accounts in ADUC, then wrote a Group Policy Object to restrict user-level system access — and validated it actually applied with a manual gpupdate /force rather than just trusting it worked.

Biggest takeaway: most of what looks like "networking magic" is just making sure two settings agree with each other. Full write-up and screenshots are in the GitHub repo.

v1.1Aug 2026

What building Mindwave at Cigna taught me about healthcare UX

Notes on writing a privacy policy page that people actually read.

Read more →

During the NEXT Program, our team built Mindwave, a mental health tracking app, and I ended up owning the Privacy Policy page. On paper that sounds like the most boring page in the app. In practice it was the one where getting the tone wrong could actually cost someone's trust.

Mental health data is about as sensitive as data gets, and most privacy policies are written like legal insurance rather than something a stressed-out user is supposed to read at 11pm. So the goal wasn't just "list what we collect" — it was making someone feel safe enough to keep using the app instead of quietly deleting it.

I built the page in React and TypeScript, but most of the actual work was editing: cutting legal boilerplate down to plain sentences, front-loading the parts people care about most (what's shared, what isn't, who can see it), and using Figma mockups to test whether the structure actually guided the eye or just buried the important parts under headings.

Presenting it to Cigna stakeholders afterward was its own lesson — explaining a design decision to non-technical stakeholders isn't that different from explaining a fix to a non-technical end user. Same skill, different room.

v1.0Jun 2026

Passing the Google IT Support Certificate: what actually helped

The modules worth slowing down on, and the ones you can move through quickly.

Read more →

I went into the Google IT Support Professional Certificate expecting it to be mostly review — I'd already been through networking and systems coursework at UMSL. It was, for some of it. But three modules were worth slowing all the way down for, because they showed up almost immediately in real troubleshooting: the networking module on subnetting and the OSI model, the systems administration module on Active Directory basics, and the section on binary/troubleshooting methodology.

That last one sounds abstract but it's the most practical thing in the whole certificate. It's essentially a framework for not guessing: identify the problem, establish a theory, test the theory, and only then implement a fix. It's the difference between restarting something five times and hoping, versus actually narrowing down what's broken.

The customer service and communication module I moved through faster — a lot of it overlapped with things I'd already picked up from retail and tutoring, where translating a problem for someone who doesn't share your vocabulary is just part of the job.

The certificate on its own doesn't make you job-ready. What made it useful was pairing it with something hands-on right after — which is part of why I built the Active Directory home lab next.
